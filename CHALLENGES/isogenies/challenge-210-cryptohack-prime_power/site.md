## Statement (verbatim, extracted 2026-10-14)

> For SIDH, we used a prime of the form p = 2^eA · 3^eB − 1 and we computed isogenies of
> degree ℓ^e by finding a point of order ℓ^e and using this as a generator.
>
> The characteristic for CSIDH primes is instead a product of many small primes with
> exponent equal to one. To compute a ℓ^e isogeny in CSIDH, one should instead compute a
> point of order ℓ, then compute the codomain of the isogeny generated by this point and
> repeat the whole process e times.

> Let's explore the structure of the ℓ-isogeny graph in CSIDH by computing repeated ℓ-

## Facts

- p = 419, p+1 = 420 = 2²·3·5·7 (CSIDH-style "small primes, exponent one" factorization of p+1).
- E0: y² = x³ + x over F_419. #E0(F_419) = 420 = p+1 → trace 0, supersingular.
- **j(E0) = 1728 ≡ 52 (mod 419)** — E0 is the mod-419 reduction of the classic j=1728 curve.
- Every curve isogenous to E0 over F_419 also has #E = 420, hence l | #E with l² ∤ #E for
  l ∈ {3,5,7}: the pointwise-rational l-torsion E(F_p)[l] is cyclic of order l, giving a
  unique "pointwise" rational l-isogeny (the CSIDH edge, kernel E(F_p)[l]).
- Note: over F_{p²} the l-torsion is full ((Z/l)², since l² | #E(F_{p²}) = p²+1+2p), so each
  curve also has a *second* (conjugate, non-pointwise) rational l-isogeny. The CSIDH walk
  uses the pointwise one; the challenge's repeated-isogeny walk does too.

## Results

**5^9 observation (first part of the statement):** iterating the pointwise 5-isogeny 9
times from E0 lands on y² = x³ + 321x, which is F_419-isomorphic to E0 (321 = 66⁴ = 353⁴
mod 419; isomorphism X = 66²X′, Y = 66³Y′). So: *the degree 5^9 isogeny comes back to the
starting curve* — the 5-isogeny graph contains a 9-cycle through E0
(j-sequence: 52 → 13 → 98 → 62 → 407 → 407 → 62 → 98 → 13 → 52, palindromic).
