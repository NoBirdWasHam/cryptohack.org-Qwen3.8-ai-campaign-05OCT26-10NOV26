## Statement (verbatim from remaining_descs.json)
> It's time to compute our first two-isogeny! In this set of challenges we will work our
> way towards implementing (and then breaking!!) the supersingular isogeny
> Diffie-Hellman protocol (SIDH).
>
> Note: for all of the following challenges we will be working with the finite field
> F_p². Where p ≡ 3 mod 4. We will use the modulus i² + 1 so elements of the field will
> be of the form x = a + ib for a, b ∈ F_p (GF(p**2, names="i", modulus=[1,0,1])).
>
> In this challenge, your goal is to compute the two-isogeny with domain E and kernel
> generated by K:
>
>   E : y² = x³ + x,   K = (i, 0),   p = 2^18 · 3^13 − 1
>
> The flag for this challenge is the j-invariant j(E′) of the codomain of this isogeny:
>   φ₂ : E → E′.
>
> Resources: Sutherland's lecture notes (Theorem 5.13 — prime degree isogenies);
> Renes, "Computing isogenies between Montgomery curves using the action of (0,0)".

## Parameters
- Curve E: y² = x³ + x  (a₁=a₂=a₃=0, a₄=1, a₆=0), j(E) = 1728 (supersingular).
- Kernel K: ⟨(i, 0)⟩ over F_p², p = 2^18·3^13 − 1 = 3399680048855967 (p ≡ 3 mod 4).
- Field: F_p² = F_p[i], i² = −1.
